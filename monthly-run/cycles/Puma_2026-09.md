## Sentiment retag (Puma only — competitors as exported, same as August)

| Step | Count |
|------|-------|
| Passing news/blog mention (title does not name the Puma) pos/neg → neutral | 27 |
| WRC race result / rally content pos/neg → neutral | 22 |
| Single-row corrections on the post itself (individually reviewed, by row) | 9 (3 → positive, 4 → negative, 2 → neutral: the passing UK policy post and a mis-tagged quote-reply) |

Result: Puma 3.9% positive (28) and 4.6% negative (33), against 3.1% and 6.8% published for August (+0.7pp / −2.2pp).
Compute pp deltas from unrounded shares, never from the rounded %s; the first restatement mis-rounded them.
As exported, after cleaning, it was 7.4% / 7.9%. Negatives are forum-led (25 of 33) and fragmented, with no cluster ≥30.
14 of the 33 negatives and 8 of the 28 positives are Brandwatch tags on asides rather than views on the car. They are
left as tagged for comparability with August and disclosed in the notes, not described as themes.

**Basis correction after the review pass.** The first build added a third bucket ("not an opinion on the Puma",
28 rows → neutral) that August never used. It took Puma negative to 2.1%, and about 2.5pp of the apparent −4.7pp
drop was method, not market. At least four rows in that bucket were real opinions on the car. The bucket was
removed. Rule: keep the retag to August's two buckets plus row-level polarity fixes, and never add a bucket in
one month only. Posts that Brandwatch tags negative but that are about policy or tax stay as tagged, and are
described in the notes.

**Trap hit this cycle:** a text-pattern polarity fix ("0/20 pour les cligno AV des Ford Puma") also matched
seven forum quote-replies that only *quote* the comment, and would have flipped all of them to negative.
Restrict polarity fixes to the row's own tag (here `positive → negative` on the one original post), never
to "all rows containing the phrase". The same applied to the "the Puma was a fine looking car" fix (two quote-replies).
